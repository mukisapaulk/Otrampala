For a news site, the palette basically sorts itself: you need one near-black for the wall of text, one near-white for the page, and the two brights strictly as accents.

**Blue Chalk `#F5F3FF` — page background.** Slightly warmer and softer than pure white, which matters when someone reads 1,200 words. Use pure white `#FFFFFF` for cards/article containers sitting on top of it so you get subtle depth without borders.

**Haiti `#18102B` — body text, headlines, header/footer.** 17.5:1 against Blue Chalk, so it's comfortably accessible. For secondary text (bylines, timestamps, captions) use Haiti at 65-70% opacity rather than a separate gray.

**Electric Violet `#834DFB` — links, active nav, section labels, primary buttons.** It's 4.55:1 on Blue Chalk, which squeaks past AA for normal text but isn't generous. Two options: darken it to around `#6B35E0` for inline links in body copy, or keep the bright version for large text, buttons (violet fill, white text), and underlines only.

**Turbo `#F0E100` — the "look here" color, used sparingly.** It's 1.3:1 on light backgrounds, so it can never be text on white. Where it works: live/breaking badges, a highlighter behind Haiti text, a thin top border on a lead story, or as text _on_ Haiti (13.4:1, very strong). One or two uses per screen max, otherwise it stops meaning "urgent."

A rough hierarchy that works:

- Masthead: Haiti background, Blue Chalk logo, Turbo dot or underline on the live-news item
- Section kickers ("Politics", "Business"): Electric Violet, uppercase, small
- Breaking banner: Haiti bar with Turbo text
- Article body: Haiti on Blue Chalk, violet links
- Newsletter signup / paywall block: Electric Violet fill, white text, Turbo button
